# Positioning: the transparency argument

## MR-32. The argument, from a real advert

A respected UK casting director, running a feature-film open call over
Instagram this summer, gave applicants the whole of her privacy position in
one sentence: all materials will be processed in accordance with GDPR
regulations. There was no controller identity, no retention period, no
statement of rights, no privacy notice, no consent record, and no route for
a parent, although the role sought 17 to 20 year olds. Applications went by
email, so unsolicited video of minors arrived in a working inbox and stayed
there for as long as the inbox did. The call also asked for heritage as a
free-text field and applied "must be Jewish or have Jewish heritage" as a
hard criterion: special category data, collected deliberately, with no
separate consent and no record of the occupational requirement that makes
the criterion lawful.

None of this is a criticism of the casting director, who was doing what
everybody does, competently. That is the point. In one advert from a
well-regarded professional there is an Article 13 failure, an Article 6
problem and a safeguarding problem, because the tools casting directors
have are email and a caption.

Open Casting is the tool that makes the ordinary advert compliant without
asking the casting director to become a lawyer. Every casting page names
the controller, says what the data is for and on what basis, gives the day
it is destroyed, lists the rights and names the ICO. A child's submission
comes through a parent, with the consent recorded. A question about
heritage or faith can be asked only with the occupational requirement
written down, under a separate consent, and the answer is held apart, read
by fewer people and deleted sooner. Briefs for children's roles are read
before they are posted. Every page says it is free and the only place to
apply, with somewhere to report an imitation.

For the Casting Directors' Guild, that is a stronger case than workflow or
review speed: the platform lets a casting director keep running the calls
they already run, and closes the gaps they did not know were open.

## MR-33. The benchmark

The studio-scale call on the incumbent platform is the functional standard
for the applicant experience: two videos with separate briefs and caps,
seven bullet points of self-tape guidance with a framing image, a
prominent anti-fraud notice, a stated deadline to the minute, and an
inclusive casting statement. Each of those now has an equivalent here, and
three of them are better placed: the guidance sits beside the upload rather
than in a caption, a tape over the limit is refused before it is sent
rather than after, and the child-safeguarding read of the brief has no
equivalent there at all. Mobile, guidance and compliance are where the
comparison is won.
